# Novelty Boundary

**Review date:** 2026-10-03

## What Is Not Novel by Itself

The following are established capabilities or active research/product categories:

- AI-guided interviews.
- Voice-to-story or memoir generation.
- Family memory archives.
- Oral-history search.
- Expert interviews.
- Tacit-knowledge extraction.
- LLM-generated knowledge bases/wikis.
- Knowledge graphs.
- Multimodal RAG.
- AI tutoring.
- Adaptive apprenticeship.
- Learner video assessment.
- Procedural skill assessment.
- Expert cognition modeling.
- Agentic workflows.
- Source citations.
- Community/cultural archives.

Current products and research include Storyworth, Remento, FamilySearch, StoryFile, SightCall, Poka, Dozuki, DeepHow, Mission Control/Bob, Tacivo, Tacit/getAbstract, Accrete Knowledge Engine, AI Expert Twin, and ProSkill.

## Current Differentiation Hypothesis

The differentiating product unit is a **knowledge transmission event**.

A transmission event connects:

**authentic expert source → structured knowledge → evidence → teaching → learner attempt → evidence-based feedback → improvement → lineage**

The system is not merely answering a question or generating a lesson.

It must show that a specific piece of knowledge moved from one human to another.

## Why This May Still Be Defensible

The differentiation is not a single model capability. It is the integration of:

1. source fidelity;
2. multimodal evidence;
3. explicit uncertainty;
4. learner practice;
5. observable verification;
6. measurable transmission;
7. adaptation/versioning;
8. lineage;
9. contributor/community governance.

## Competitive Falsification Test

Before submission, attempt to reproduce the complete MVP using the strongest relevant competitor.

If an existing product can demonstrate the same:

- source capture,
- source-grounded teaching,
- learner attempt analysis,
- evidence-linked feedback,
- measurable improvement,
- transmission lineage,

then the differentiation claim must be revised.

## Positioning Rule

Do not say:

> "Nobody does this."

Say:

> "Our prototype combines these capabilities around a measurable human-to-human transmission event, and we preserve the source and lineage across that event."


## Skills of the physical world (positioning, DECIDED 2026-10-07)

This section is the single statement of Anveshan's positioning. Other documents and the app link to it or adapt its wording; they do not restate it.

**One line (use as written in the README, deck and video):**

> Anveshan passes on skills of the physical world from one person to another: learned from a real demonstration, practised with real hands, and checked against a photo, video or recording of the learner's real attempt.

Skills of the physical world are done with hands, body, tools, materials and sound: folding, drawing, drumming, repairing, weaving. Text cannot carry them well, and a chat tutor cannot see them. That is where Anveshan is focused, and what follows from it:

1. **The source is the real thing.** A skill starts from a real demonstration or a real finished piece (photo, video or audio), not from text written about it.
2. **The attempt is the real thing.** The learner's evidence is what they physically did, captured with an ordinary phone: a photo of the drawing, a video of their hands, a recording of their playing. No sensors or special kit.
3. **Feedback points back to the physical moment.** Each tip links to the place in the demonstration (a frame, a time, a region) that shows what to change, starting from the first point where the attempt went off track.
4. **Honest about what a camera or microphone cannot sense.** Force, pressure, torque and touch are not measured; the app says "couldn't tell" rather than guessing.
5. **Transfer is measured in the physical result.** The retry is checked the same way and the change is recorded.

**Contrast (wording for copy and pitch; positioning, not validated evidence):**

- Chat tutors and text courses explain a skill but cannot see what your hands did.
- Video courses show the skill but never look back at your attempt.
- Industrial "Physical AI" tools (for example DeepHow, see `docs/research/competitive-red-team-addendum-2026-10-03.md`) target enterprise procedures. Anveshan is for anyone with a phone and any craft, keeps the creator's credit and consent, and measures whether the skill transferred.

The positioning rule above still applies: say "made for skills of the physical world", never "nobody does this".
