# Solution-Space Exploration — 2026-10-03\n\n> Exploration only. No product thesis, domain, architecture, or MVP direction is locked.\n\n## Purpose\n\nWe are reopening the Anveshan search space rather than optimizing the current thesis. The objective is to discover what a three-person team can build for Google Cloud AI Builder Cup 2026 that combines real user value, strong GenAI implementation, visible technical depth, differentiation, measurable outcomes, and a reliable three-minute demonstration.\n\nThe official judging weights are Technical Merit & Gen AI Implementation 40%, Problem Alignment & Impact 25%, Innovation & Creativity 25%, and User Experience & Solution Design 10%. Official themes include BFSI, Retail & Commerce, Manufacturing, Media/Content/Digital Experiences, Future of Work & Enterprise Productivity, and Sustainability & Social Impact.\n\n## Strategic reset\n\nDo not ask: How do we make Anveshan better?\n\nAsk: Given current multimodal, agentic, embodied-reasoning, simulation, and world-model capabilities, what real problem is newly solvable?\n\nExisting concepts remain candidates, not commitments.\n\n## New Google capability signal\n\nGoogle DeepMind currently places Genie 3 and Gemini Robotics 2 in a World models & physical AI category. Genie 3 is described as a general-purpose world model that generates interactive environments and models how environments evolve and how actions affect them. Google describes real-time interaction, short-horizon state persistence, and promptable world events, while explicitly listing limitations including limited action space, imperfect real-world-location simulation, multi-agent interaction challenges, and only a few minutes of continuous interaction.\n\nGemini Robotics ER 2 is described as an embodied-reasoning model for understanding physical environments, multi-step planning, tool use, success detection, and coordination. Its model card lists text, image, video and audio inputs and spatial, temporal and physical reasoning.\n\nImplication: the search space is no longer just chatbots, RAG, document intelligence, or generic agents. We should investigate products where AI understands changing real-world state, actions, and outcomes.\n\n## Capability primitives\n\n### Perception\n- image and video understanding\n- temporal localization\n- object and spatial understanding\n- audio and speech\n- documents\n- multimodal embeddings\n\n### Reasoning\n- planning\n- state estimation\n- success detection\n- uncertainty\n- counterfactual reasoning\n- tool use\n\n### Agency\n- multi-step execution\n- retrieval\n- memory\n- evaluation\n- orchestration\n\n### Physical intelligence\n- spatial reasoning\n- temporal reasoning\n- physical reasoning\n- action planning\n- human demonstration learning\n- robot interaction\n\n### Simulation\n- interactive environments\n- controllable world events\n- scenario generation\n- agent training\n\n### Human learning\n- tutoring\n- coaching\n- apprenticeship\n- assessment\n- adaptive practice\n\n### Collective knowledge\n- source aggregation\n- provenance\n- variants\n- contradictions\n- lineage\n\n## Candidate thesis families\n\n### A. Human Skill World Model\nConstruct a structured representation of a real-world skill: objects, actions, states, transitions, conditions, cues, decisions, outcomes, exceptions and variants. The unit is not a text chunk but state → action → transition → outcome.\n\nPotential killer moment: a demonstration is converted into a skill-state model and the system answers a counterfactual question while distinguishing observed evidence from inference.\n\nRisk: true causal inference is difficult. Do not claim a general world model for a narrow procedural representation.\n\n### B. AI Apprentice\nThe AI learns from a human instead of teaching the human. Human demonstrates → AI forms a hypothesis → human corrects → AI revises → AI handles a new case.\n\nKiller moment: the expert visibly corrects an AI hypothesis and the system changes its structured representation.\n\nRisk: it can degenerate into a prompt loop unless the evolving hypothesis and measurable improvement are explicit.\n\n### C. Collective Skill Memory\nUse permitted public, licensed, or first-party demonstrations to understand how different humans perform the same skill. Compare variants, common behaviors, context dependencies and disagreements.\n\nKiller moment: multiple legitimate demonstrations are reconciled into a map of invariant and context-dependent practices.\n\nRisks: copyright, source authority, attribution, contradictory practices, and platform access.\n\n### D. AI Skill Referee\nObserve a learner performing a procedure and evaluate against a human-authored rubric. Output criterion-level observations, temporal evidence, uncertainty and targeted feedback.\n\nThis is an active research area. ProSkill 2026 introduced a benchmark for segment-level procedural skill assessment and reports continuing difficulty for current methods. Therefore generic video scoring is not sufficient differentiation.\n\n### E. Counterfactual Coach\nMove from descriptive feedback to action/outcome reasoning: what changes if the learner changes an action, sequence, condition, tool, or parameter?\n\nRisk: causal claims must be separated from observed correlations and expert convention.\n\n### F. Skill Variant Engine\nGiven multiple demonstrations, identify invariants, valid variants, context-dependent adaptations, disputed practices and failure modes. The product does not assume one canonical answer.\n\n### G. Failure Intelligence\nModel failed and near-miss attempts, not just successful demonstrations. Learn failure signatures, earliest divergence points, expert interventions and recovery paths.\n\nPotential advantage: failure evidence is directly actionable for training.\n\n### H. Simulation Apprenticeship\nTurn procedural knowledge into an interactive practice environment. Learners perform rather than watch. The system introduces variation and evaluates outcomes.\n\nWorld-model technology makes this direction newly interesting, but current Genie 3 limitations and availability mean it should not be treated as a dependable MVP dependency without verification.\n\n### I. Physical-World Inspector\nUse camera/video plus structured procedures to understand physical state, identify deviations, recommend an action, and verify the result. Potential themes include Manufacturing, Retail and Sustainability.\n\nRisk: visual inspection is crowded. The wedge must be more specific than generic anomaly detection.\n\n### J. Human-to-Robot Skill Transfer\nHuman demonstrates a physical task; AI extracts task structure; robot executes it. This directly aligns with Google's Robotics direction.\n\nRisk: hardware and reliability. Explore aggressively, but do not make it the default without a credible robot path.\n\n### K. Demonstration Compiler\nCompile messy human demonstrations into machine-readable, executable knowledge: states, actions, conditions, branches, tools and verification criteria.\n\nPotentially powerful because the resulting representation could drive humans, agents, simulations and eventually robots.\n\n### L. Skill Provenance / Git for Practice\nRepresent the evolution of a procedure: who changed it, what evidence supported the change, why it changed, and under what context the new variant works.\n\n### M. Rare-Event Simulator\nCreate safe training for unusual physical situations: equipment failures, supply disruptions, environmental scenarios, inspection edge cases and other expensive-to-reproduce events.\n\n### N. AI Work-State Engine\nUnderstand work as changing state rather than as documents. Observe physical and digital evidence, estimate current state, identify deviation, recommend action, and verify completion.\n\n### O. Repair Intelligence\nObserve a damaged object, diagnose likely repair paths, guide the repair, and verify the result. This has a direct sustainability narrative.\n\n### P. Financial Event Reconstruction\nApply the same state/action/evidence abstraction to fragmented financial events: transactions, statements, documents and communications become a grounded event timeline with inconsistencies and investigation paths.\n\n### Q. Interactive Documentary / Historical World\nCombine source-grounded evidence with interactive environments. Users explore rather than merely watch. This is visually strong but needs a clear impact thesis.\n\n## Cross-direction insight\n\nAcross the strongest ideas, the recurring abstraction is:\n\nSTATE → ACTION → OUTCOME\n\nA conventional knowledge system models information. A world model models changing state. A skill system models human action and outcome. A field agent models physical and task state. A manufacturing system models production state. A repair system models object state.\n\nThis may be more fundamental than the current labels of agent, RAG, knowledge graph, or tutor.\n\n## Three strategic axes\n\n### Reality\nHow grounded is the product in an actual physical or operational environment?\ndocuments → video → physical scene → interactive environment\n\n### State\nDoes the AI merely describe things, or model current state, action, transition and resulting state?\n\n### Agency\nDoes the system answer, recommend, teach, simulate, or act?\n\nThe most strategically interesting area may be the intersection of real-world grounding, state modeling, and agency.\n\n## Ideas we should treat as weak by default\n\n- generic chatbot\n- generic RAG\n- PDF assistant\n- meeting summarizer\n- generic AI tutor\n- generic AI interviewer\n- generic knowledge graph\n- enterprise search wrapper\n- generic video summarizer\n- generic agent without a novel workflow\n- generic image inspection\n- generic skill score\n- generic digital human\n- generic oral-history archive\n- generic SOP generator\n\nThese are not forbidden; they simply require unusually strong differentiation to survive.\n\n## Research questions before convergence\n\n1. Which physical-AI workflows have low competition but strong measurable outcomes?\n4. Which problems require multimodal temporal reasoning rather than ordinary LLM reasoning?\n5. Which directions map cleanly to one official AI Builder Cup theme?\n\n## Convergence test\n\nDo not select a product until we can complete this sentence:\n\nBefore current multimodal/agentic/physical AI capabilities, this problem required ______. Now our system can ______, which changes ______.\n\nIf the answer is merely faster text generation, reject it.\n\nIf the answer involves understanding dynamic state, reasoning about actions and outcomes, intervening, and verifying the resulting state, investigate it deeply.\n\n## Required next phase\n\nBefore implementation, produce 3–5 genuinely different product theses. For each, document:\n\n- user and problem\n- why existing solutions are inadequate\n- breakthrough capability\n- Google capability used\n- falsifiable technical hypothesis\n- measurable outcome\n- closest competitors\n- copyability\n- data requirements\n- hardware requirements\n- MVP boundary\n- three-minute demo\n- killer technical moment\n- failure mode\n- safety/legal/privacy risks\n- expansion path\n- evidence required to justify the thesis\n\nThen run a red-team review whose instruction is explicitly: kill the ideas if necessary; do not protect the existing Anveshan thesis.\n\n## Current position\n\nWe are not ready to select the skill.\nWe are not ready to freeze transmission as the product outcome.\nWe are not ready to freeze the current agent architecture.\n\nThe strongest new hypothesis to investigate is the broader transition from AI that knows information to AI that understands changing states of the real world and reasons about actions and outcomes.\n\nHuman expertise, video, skill models, world models, simulation, agents and robotics should all remain candidate components rather than predetermined product features.\n\n## Sources and evidence\n\n- Google AI Builder Cup 2026 official themes and judging: https://aibuildercup.com/themes.html\n- Google DeepMind, Genie 3: https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/\n- Google DeepMind, Genie 3 model page: https://deepmind.google/models/genie/\n- Google DeepMind, Gemini Robotics ER 2: https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-robotics/embodied-reasoning/\n- Google DeepMind, Gemini Robotics 2: https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-robotics/\n- Gemini Robotics ER 2 model card: https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/gemini-robotics-er-2/\n- WACV 2026 ProSkill: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Mazzamuto_ProSkill_Segment-Level_Skill_Assessment_in_Procedural_Videos_WACV_2026_paper.html
