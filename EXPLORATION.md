# Anveshan Exploration Record

**Purpose:** Preserve the complete reasoning trail that led to the current Anveshan thesis. This is deliberately not a polished product document. It is the project's institutional memory.

## 1. Starting Point

The original concept was centered on capturing the stories and knowledge of elders/grandparents. The initial mental model was an AI that interviews grandparents, asks follow-up questions, and turns their stories and memories into a family archive.

Initial value propositions included preserving memories before they disappear, helping younger generations hear stories from elders, creating searchable family history, preserving voice and personality, turning conversations into memoirs, connecting stories to people/places/events, multilingual access, and sharing memories across generations.

## 2. Why the Original Family-Memory Direction Was Not Enough

Competitive research showed that AI-guided memory capture, voice-first memoir creation, family archives, genealogy, and conversational access to recorded historical material are already active categories.

**Storyworth:** AI-guided Magic Interviews and voice-based story capture. Official sources: https://help.storyworth.com/en_US/magic-interviews and https://help.storyworth.com/can-i-voice-record-my-stories

**Remento:** voice-first memory capture, spoken memories turned into books, with original recordings accessible through QR-linked experiences. Official source: https://www.remento.co/

**FamilySearch:** photos, stories, documents, audio, family relationships, family-tree context, search and uploads. Official source: https://www.familysearch.org/

**StoryFile:** interactive access to authentic recorded responses, including institutional and historical applications. Official source: https://www.storyfile.com/

Strategic lesson: a better grandparent interviewer, memoir generator, family archive, or family-tree chatbot would not create enough differentiation for the hackathon.

## 3. First Strategic Shift: Stories to Knowledge

The project moved from preserving stories toward preserving and transmitting knowledge.

The important insight was that valuable human knowledge is not necessarily a story. It may be a skill, practice, heuristic, sensory cue, exception, decision rule, workaround, local observation, timing judgment, or embodied technique.

Examples explored included how a craftsperson knows a material is ready; how a cook adjusts technique based on smell, texture, temperature, or weather; how a farmer decides when to act; how a musician notices a subtle timing error; how an experienced worker recognizes an abnormal condition; how someone knows when a standard procedure should not be followed; local environmental observations; practical heuristics; exceptions; workarounds; and judgment developed through experience.

This led to the concept of tacit knowledge.

## 4. Tacit Knowledge Exploration

Potential tacit-knowledge categories included implicit rules, intuition, judgment, pattern recognition, sensory cues, timing, thresholds, exceptions, edge cases, environmental indicators, decision heuristics, failure recognition, expert shortcuts, undocumented workarounds, context-dependent behavior, when-not-to rules, and reasons behind procedures.

Important correction: Anveshan should not claim that AI magically understands all tacit knowledge. A more defensible formulation is: **Anveshan makes observable parts of tacit expertise explicit and source-grounded.**

## 5. Enterprise and Industrial Knowledge Transfer

The next direction was capturing the knowledge of experienced workers before retirement. The conceptual loop was expert → demonstration → AI extraction → knowledge model → learner → practice → feedback → transfer.

Potential applications included manufacturing, field service, maintenance, construction, agriculture, professional expertise, institutional knowledge, and retirement knowledge transfer.

Deeper research showed that this market is already substantially populated.

SightCall supports expert capture, visual support, AI structuring, and reusable knowledge for service interactions. In October 2026 it announced Xpert Knowledge APIs and MCP integrations for expert service interactions and procedures.

Sources: https://sightcall.com/solutions/field-service/ and https://sightcall.com/blog/sightcall-expert-app-xk-integrations-launch/

Lesson: expert interaction → reusable knowledge is already an active product category.

### Poka

Poka's Knowledge Flywheel focuses on capturing experienced operator knowledge and making it available to frontline workers.

Sources: https://www.poka.io/en/connected-worker-platform/ and https://www.poka.io/blog/poka-plants-the-flag-on-industrial-ai-for-connected-work/

Lesson: capturing retiring operator knowledge alone is not enough differentiation.

### Dozuki

Dozuki uses AI to convert frontline demonstrations into work instructions and learning pathways and supports visual verification.

Source: https://www.dozuki.com/how-to-use-ai-standard-work

Lesson: demonstration → procedure/instruction is already productized.

### DeepHow

DeepHow combines instruction, guided practice, and verified performance, including visual process inspection.

Source: https://deephow.com/use-cases/training-within-industry

Lesson: AI apprenticeship, video training, and verification are increasingly populated categories.

### Mission Control / Bob

Mission Control's Bob captures first-person expert video, audio, spatial information, and reasoning while an expert works.

Source: https://www.usemissioncontrol.com/bob/manufacturing/

Lesson: first-person expert capture is also an emerging category.

## 6. Strategic Conclusion from Industrial Research

Anveshan should not become 'Katha for factories'. Industrial tacit knowledge transfer is too crowded to serve as the primary novelty wedge. Enterprise functionality can remain a future expansion.

## 7. Cultural Preservation Exploration

The project returned to cultural and community knowledge: traditional crafts, food traditions, agriculture, ecological practices, oral histories, music and dance, local practices, regional techniques, and community memory.

Research showed that this area is also evolving rapidly.

### Government of India

Current initiatives around culture and AI include digitization, translation, access, and dissemination.

Source: https://culture.gov.in/events/ministry-culture-showcases-confluence-heritage-and-technology-india-ai-impact-summit-2026

Lesson: AI-enabled cultural preservation is strategically relevant, but a generic AI cultural archive is unlikely to be sufficiently novel.

### AI4Bharat Smriti

Smriti supports authorized digital archivists collecting multimodal cultural data including audio, video, and photographs.

Source: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_IN&id=org.ai4bharat.smriti

Lesson: multimodal cultural data collection already exists.

### Bharat Sanskriti

A community project uses multimodal AI for cultural discovery, oral history, and preservation.

Source: https://github.com/ShruthiPakanati/Bharat-Sankriti

Lesson: cultural discovery plus multimodal AI is not an empty category.

### Academic work

Research in 2026 includes multimodal RAG for traditional knowledge preservation and transmission.

Source: https://academic.oup.com/dsh/article-abstract/41/2/1138/8643957

Systematic reviews have highlighted a risk that technology-heavy projects can create digital fossilization when communities are not meaningfully involved.

Sources: https://www.nature.com/articles/s40494-026-02458-y and https://iaiai.org/letters/index.php/liir/article/view/506

Lesson: community participation and governance must be part of the product model, not an afterthought.

## 8. UNESCO / Living Heritage Insight

UNESCO's framing of intangible cultural heritage emphasizes that living heritage is community-based, continually recreated, transmitted across generations, and contextual rather than static.

Sources: https://ich.unesco.org/en/transmission-00078, https://ich.unesco.org/en/about-01159, https://ich.unesco.org/en/what-is-intangible-cultural-heritage-00003, and the 2026 report at https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/04_GLO_ED_Report_EN.pdf?v=1770196346

Lesson: a system that merely freezes knowledge into an archive can lose the thing it is trying to preserve. Evolution and transmission matter.

## 9. Oral History Ethics

Research into Oral History Association principles reinforced informed consent, contributor control, review, attribution, contextualization, access controls, preserving original source material, avoiding unsupported interpretation, and respecting the narrator's authority over their contribution.

Sources: https://oralhistory.org/oha-statement-on-ethics/, https://oralhistory.org/oha-core-principles/, https://oralhistory.org/best-practices/, and https://oralhistory.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025-OHA-Principles-and-Best-Practices-Complete.pdf

Lesson: provenance, consent, contributor review, and community governance are product requirements.

## 10. Second Strategic Shift: Preservation to Transmission

The project moved from preserving cultural knowledge toward transmitting living human expertise.

Culture became a potential first proof domain rather than the complete product definition.

Current thesis: **Anveshan is an AI transmission system for tacit human expertise.**

More defensible version: **Anveshan helps communities preserve and transmit living, embodied knowledge from one person to the next using multimodal AI.**

## 11. AI Apprenticeship Exploration

The next concept was: if AI can extract knowledge, can it teach the learner?

This produced the loop Observe → Understand → Practice → Attempt → Feedback → Retry → Demonstrate.

Potential functionality included adaptive lessons, demonstrations, practice tasks, learner uploads, AI comparison, feedback, retries, and competency evidence.

Research showed that AI apprenticeship itself is not sufficiently novel. Relevant work includes 2026 studies on LLMs as real-time tutors for hands-on construction training, generative AI in apprenticeship, and multimodal tacit skill transfer.

Sources: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14780771261454100, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/18724981251397523, and https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-17656-0

Lesson: AI tutor for traditional skills is not enough. Differentiation must be the complete source-grounded transmission system.

## 12. Current Core Insight

Knowledge should not end at documentation. It should have a transmission lifecycle.

The system should connect:

**Expert → Knowledge → Evidence → Teaching → Learner → Attempt → Feedback → Verification → Adaptation → Next learner**

This led to the concept of a Knowledge Transmission Record.

A transmission record can include expert, knowledge, original evidence, interpretation, teaching experience, learner, learner attempt, feedback, adaptation, successful transfer, and next-generation lineage.

## 13. Core Product Loop

1. **DEMONSTRATE** — the expert shows and/or explains what they know.
2. **UNDERSTAND** — multimodal AI analyzes speech, actions, objects, sequence, context, and conditions.
3. **GROUND** — claims remain connected to original evidence.
4. **TEACH** — the knowledge becomes an adaptive learning experience.
5. **PRACTICE** — the learner performs the task.
6. **VERIFY** — the learner's observable attempt is compared against evidence.
7. **EVOLVE** — successful adaptations and variants become part of the lineage.

## 14. Atomic Unit

Several possible atomic units were explored: story, document, lesson, skill, and claim. The current preferred conceptual unit is **Transmission** because it connects knowledge to people and observable learning.

## 15. Knowledge Representation

The emerging representation is a **Multimodal Knowledge Transmission Graph**.

Potential nodes: Person, Community, Family, Expert, Learner, Skill, Practice, Technique, Story, Event, Place, Region, Object, Tool, Material, Document, Recording, Photo, Video, Claim, Evidence, Lesson, Attempt, Outcome, Variant, Version, Transmission.

Potential relationships: knows, demonstrates, taught, learned, learned_from, taught_to, contains, supports, contradicts, corroborates, depends_on, precedes, follows, variant_of, adapted_from, adapted_by, inspired_by, replaced_by, related_to, attempted, evaluated_against, transmitted_to.

## 16. Source vs Interpretation

The system must distinguish source statement, demonstrated evidence, AI interpretation, community interpretation, external evidence, and unknown.

This prevents AI from silently transforming interpretation into fact.

## 17. Uncertainty and Contradiction

Living knowledge can legitimately have multiple versions. The system should preserve variants, regional differences, historical differences, expert disagreements, contextual differences, and unresolved contradictions.

Useful responses include: 'According to this practitioner'; 'Two sources describe different methods'; 'These practices differ by region'; 'The available evidence is insufficient to determine'; and 'This interpretation is AI-generated and has not been verified.'

## 18. Provenance

Every important knowledge claim should ideally be traceable to claim → recording → timestamp → speaker → language → context → location → evidence → interpretation → verification state.

This led to the concept of an Evidence Graph.

## 19. Knowledge Scout

A major additional capability is an AI Knowledge Scout that identifies what is missing rather than only answering questions.

It could discover undocumented knowledge, poorly documented knowledge, single-source knowledge, conflicting knowledge, missing context, missing steps, missing experts, unanswered questions, unexplained assumptions, potential knowledge holders, missing evidence, and missing variants.

Workflow: **Discover → Analyze → Find gap → Identify holder → Generate questions → Interview → Update graph**

## 20. Knowledge Continuity

Knowledge can become fragile when only one person knows it.

Potential signals include single-person dependency, aging knowledge holder, no successor, undocumented practice, low transmission activity, weak source diversity, and restricted access.

The system could recommend a transmission event.

## 21. Community Ownership

Potential access levels: private, family, community, approved learners, institutional, public, research-only.

Potential controls: contributor approval, community approval, correction, contextual notes, revocation, sensitive knowledge restrictions, sacred/restricted knowledge handling.

## 22. Authenticity Principle

Preserve original voice, video, images, documents, demonstrations, and language wherever permissions allow. AI summaries are secondary.

The system should not fabricate authoritative representations of deceased people or invent statements/memories.

## 23. Initial Domain Exploration

Candidate domains: traditional craft, food/culinary heritage, agriculture, fishing, manufacturing, field service, healthcare, disaster resilience, construction, music/dance, local ecological knowledge, and enterprise retirement knowledge.

Current recommendation: use one real practitioner and one bounded skill in a domain the team can access authentically. The exact skill should not be invented before confirming data access.

## 24. Domain Selection Criteria

Prioritize authentic expert access, authentic learner access, visual richness, tacit/conditional knowledge, observable output, recordable demonstrations, measurable learner attempts, emotional resonance, technical richness, safety, judge comprehension, and expansion potential.

Avoid high-stakes healthcare and emergency use in the MVP. Avoid industrial/enterprise as the primary novelty wedge because of competitive density.

## 25. Technical Validation

Experiment 1: real expert video → extract meaningful procedural, conditional, sensory, and contextual knowledge.

Experiment 2: ask ten difficult questions → answer using exact evidence and timestamps.

Experiment 3: create a useful practice task from extracted knowledge.

Experiment 4: record a learner attempt → produce defensible observable feedback.

Experiment 5: expert reviews the representation → confirms or rejects accuracy.

Experiment 6: learner retries → measure improvement.

## 26. Kill Criteria

A. Expert says extraction misrepresents practice.
B. Video understanding cannot locate relevant evidence.
C. Feedback is generic rather than evidence-based.
D. Expert/AI disagreement is too high.
E. Demo is indistinguishable from an ordinary AI tutor.
F. No authentic data access.
G. The 90-second demo requires too much explanation.

## 27. Current Positioning

Working statement: **Anveshan helps communities preserve and transmit living, embodied knowledge from one person to the next using multimodal AI.**

Technical statement: **Anveshan is a source-grounded multimodal knowledge transmission system that captures demonstrations, structures human expertise, turns it into adaptive apprenticeship, evaluates learner attempts, and preserves the resulting lineage and evolution.**

Short statement: **Capture it. Ground it. Teach it. Practice it. Pass it on.**

## 28. What Anveshan Is Not

Not primarily a family memoir app, grandparent interview app, genealogy platform, family tree, story generator, oral-history recorder, cultural archive, industrial knowledge-management system, retiring-worker knowledge archive, generic AI tutor, generic chatbot, document RAG system, AI avatar of an elder, or synthetic historical-person simulation.

Individual features from these categories may be useful, but none should define the product.

## 29. Current Strategic Thesis

The exploration path was: **Stories → Memories → Oral history → Tacit knowledge → Enterprise knowledge transfer → Cultural knowledge → Apprenticeship → Provenance → Transmission → Lineage**.

The current thesis is: **Human knowledge is valuable not merely because it can be stored, but because it can be transmitted.**

Therefore: **the fundamental product unit is not a story, document, or lesson. It is a knowledge transmission between people, grounded in evidence and capable of evolving without losing lineage.**

# 31. 2026-10-03 Red-Team Research Checkpoint

A fresh panel review was conducted across product strategy, AI architecture, learning science, competitive intelligence, cultural governance, UX and hackathon judging.

The review materially changed the project in four ways.

## 31.1 Novelty Was Narrowed

AI interviewing, tacit-knowledge extraction, knowledge graphs, multimodal RAG, AI tutoring, apprenticeship, learner video assessment and agentic architecture are all active areas.

The project therefore no longer treats any of these as standalone novelty.

The current differentiation hypothesis is the complete transmission event:

**authentic expert source → grounded knowledge → teaching → learner attempt → evidence-linked verification → measurable improvement → lineage**

## 31.2 Competitive Research Was Expanded

Newly incorporated direct/adjacent competitors and research include Tacivo, Tacit/getAbstract, Accrete Knowledge Engine, AI Expert Twin and ProSkill/ProSkill++.

This further weakens the case for a generic enterprise tacit-knowledge platform or generic AI apprenticeship product.

## 31.3 Skill Evaluation Became a First-Class Research Problem

Current 2026 research shows that procedural video skill assessment is possible but difficult. The product therefore should not return a single unconstrained AI skill score.

Instead, the evaluation architecture should use observable criteria, source/reference segments, criterion-level evidence, uncertainty, and independent expert review.

## 31.4 Governance Became More Explicit

2026 work on Indigenous data sovereignty, community governance, UNESCO data commons and oral-history AI ethics reinforces that community authority cannot be claimed merely because a product contains permissions.

If the MVP uses community-held knowledge, governance must be designed with the relevant community. Otherwise, the product should make a narrower contributor-controlled/permission-aware claim.

## 31.5 Current Product Statement

The strongest current formulation is:

> **Anveshan turns an expert's real demonstration into evidence-grounded apprenticeship, watches the learner's attempt, and records whether the knowledge actually transferred.**

This is now the primary product statement for further validation.
